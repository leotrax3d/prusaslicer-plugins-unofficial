# Terminal Precision

A visual language for things that are measured before they are trusted. It borrows the calm
of a console at night: near-black ground, warm off-white text, and one acid-lime signal that
only appears where something has been proven. Everything else stays quiet so that the one
lit value can be read from across the room.

Space is a grid you can feel but rarely see. A field of faint dots sits under every
composition like the cells of a print bed or a sheet of graph paper. Forms snap to it with
painstaking exactness. Margins are generous, alignments are absolute, and nothing floats
without a reason. The result should look meticulously crafted, as if every coordinate was
typed by hand and checked twice.

Form is drawn, not illustrated: hairline strokes, true right angles, technical-drawing
restraint. Objects appear as calibrated specimens with dimension ticks, index numbers and
small reference marks, like plates in a lab notebook kept by someone at the top of their
field. Solid fills are rare and deliberate; the lime fill marks the single answer a
specimen is meant to produce.

Type is monospaced throughout, because every character occupies the same honest width.
Headlines are set in uppercase at a monumental scale, with one line solid and another
reduced to an outline, as though rendered and not yet rendered. Labels are tiny, tracked
wide and muted, whispering coordinates rather than explaining. Text is never a paragraph;
it is a reading on an instrument.

Rhythm comes from repetition with small, exact differences: five bands that step evenly,
six circles that grow by fixed increments. The eye compares rather than reads. This kind
of patient accumulation is what makes the work feel like the product of deep expertise and
countless hours of refinement, master-level execution where the precision itself is the
ornament.
